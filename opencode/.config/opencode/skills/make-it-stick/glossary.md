# Glossary: Make It Stick

**Analytical Intelligence** — The ability to complete problem-solving tasks typical of standardized tests (Ch 6).

**Anti-patterns** — Ineffective learning behaviors like rereading, massed practice, and cramming that create illusions of mastery (Ch 1, 2).

**Blocked Practice** — Practicing one skill or problem type repeatedly before moving to the next; often mistaken for varied practice but less effective (Ch 3).

**Calibration** — The process of aligning your subjective sense of what you know with objective evidence of your mastery, typically through testing (Ch 5, 8).

**Cognitive Multipliers** — Strategies or behaviors (like growth mindset or mnemonics) that amplify the performance of existing intelligence (Ch 7).

**Consolidation** — The brain's process of stabilizing and strengthening memory traces, giving them meaning and connecting them to prior knowledge (Ch 4).

**Creative Intelligence** — The ability to synthesize knowledge and apply it to novel or unusual situations (Ch 6).

**Crystallized Intelligence** — One's accumulated store of knowledge and mental models built through experience (Ch 6, 7).

**Curse of Knowledge** — The tendency for experts to underestimate the difficulty of a task for a novice or to forget the component steps of a complex skill (Ch 5).

**Desirable Difficulties** — Short-term impediments that make learning feel harder but lead to stronger long-term retention and better transfer (Ch 4).

**Discrimination Skills** — The ability to assess a situation and select the correct tool or solution from a range of possibilities (Ch 3).

**Dunning-Kruger Effect** — A cognitive bias where incompetent people lack the skills to recognize their own incompetence, leading to overinflated self-assessments (Ch 5).

**Dynamic Testing** — Using tests to identify specific weaknesses and then focusing learning efforts on those areas before re-testing (Ch 6).

**Elaboration** — The process of giving new material meaning by expressing it in your own words and connecting it with what you already know (Ch 1, 8).

**Encoding** — The initial process of converting sensory perceptions into chemical and electrical memory traces in the brain (Ch 4).

**Failure (as information)** — Viewing errors not as an indictment of ability but as a source of information about what needs further work (Ch 4, 7).

**Fluency Illusions** — The false sense of mastery that comes from being able to read a text easily or becoming familiar with its wording (Ch 5).

**Fluid Intelligence** — The ability to reason, see relationships, and think abstractly in the moment, independent of prior knowledge (Ch 6, 7).

**Generation Effect** — The phenomenon where producing an answer or solving a problem yourself (even if you make mistakes) strengthens learning more than being given the answer (Ch 2, 4).

**Growth Mindset** — The belief that intellectual ability is not fixed but can be developed through effort, strategy, and learning (Ch 7).

**Habit Strength** — The durable, long-term retention of a skill or knowledge built through effortful practice, as opposed to "momentary strength" (Ch 3).

**Illusions of Knowing** — Various cognitive biases that mislead us into thinking we have mastered material when we have not (Ch 1, 5).

**Interleaved Practice** — Mixing the practice of different but related topics or problem types within a single session (Ch 1, 3).

**Lability** — The unstable, modifiable state of new learning before it has been fully consolidated or when it is retrieved (Ch 4).

**Leitner System** — A method of spaced repetition using flashcard boxes to vary the frequency of review based on mastery (Ch 3).

**Memory Palace (Method of Loci)** — A mnemonic device where you associate vivid mental images with specific locations in a familiar physical environment (Ch 7).

**Mental Models** — Integrated sets of perceptions and actions that are bundled together into efficient "brain apps" for responding to complex situations (Ch 1, 4).

**Metacognition** — "Thinking about thinking"; the process of monitoring one's own level of understanding and performance (Ch 1, 5).

**Mnemonics** — Mental tools or systems (like acronyms or images) used to help organize and retrieve large volumes of information (Ch 7, 8).

**Neuroplasticity** — The brain's capacity to physically change its structure and organization throughout life in response to learning (Ch 7).

**Practical Intelligence** — "Street smarts"; the ability to adapt to the demands of everyday life and understand what needs to be done in a specific setting (Ch 6).

**Reconsolidation** — The process where retrieving a memory makes it "pliable" again, allowing it to be updated and more deeply entrenched (Ch 4).

**Reflection** — A combination of retrieval and elaboration where you review an experience and ask questions to improve future performance (Ch 2, 8).

**Retrieval Practice** — The act of calling information to mind from memory; the most effective way to make learning stick (Ch 1, 2).

**Rule Learning** — Habitually extracting underlying principles or rules from new experiences rather than just memorizing examples (Ch 6).

**Spaced Practice** — Breaking study or practice into separate sessions with time intervals between them to allow for forgetting and consolidation (Ch 1, 3).

**Structure Building** — The act of extracting salient ideas and constructing a coherent mental framework out of new material (Ch 6).

**System 1** — The fast, automatic, and intuitive system of thinking; powerful but prone to illusion (Ch 5).

**System 2** — The slow, controlled, and analytical system of thinking used to check impulses and manage complex decisions (Ch 5).

**Testing Effect** — The phenomenon where taking a test on material leads to better long-term retention than spending the same time restudying (Ch 2).

**Varied Practice** — Practicing a skill in different contexts or with different parameters to build a more flexible "vocabulary" of responses (Ch 3).
