## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. Retrieval Practice (The Testing Effect)
Use retrieval as your primary learning tool. The act of calling information to mind from memory ("testing") strengthens neural pathways and "ties the knot" to prevent forgetting.
- **Prefer**: Self-quizzing and active recall over rereading or highlighting.
- **Effect**: Effortful retrieval makes the memory more durable and pliable for future use.

### 2. Spaced Retrieval
Allow some time to elapse between practice sessions so that a little forgetting occurs. This makes the subsequent retrieval more effortful and triggers memory reconsolidation.
- **Prefer**: Multiple short sessions over one long "massed" session (cramming).
- **Rule**: If retrieval is too easy, the learning isn't sticking.

### 3. Interleaving and Variation
Mix up the practice of different but related topics or skills. This forces the brain to discriminate between problem types and select the right solution.
- **Prefer**: Mixed practice (ABC, BCA) over blocked practice (AAA, BBB).
- **Effect**: Improves the ability to transfer learning to new, novel contexts.

### 4. Desirable Difficulties
Embrace the frustration of learning. Impediments that slow down apparent acquisition (like spacing, interleaving, and generation) produce stronger long-term retention.
- **Mental Model**: "Easy learning is like writing in sand." Durable learning requires building infrastructure in the brain.

### 5. Growth Mindset
Intelligence is not fixed at birth. The brain is plastic (neuroplasticity) and physically changes in response to effortful learning and challenge.
- **Mental Model**: Think of the brain as a muscle that strengthens with work, or an "infant nation" building its roads and cities.
