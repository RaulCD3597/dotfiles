## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Zettelkasten (Slip-box) System
The slip-box is an external thinking tool that relies on **bi-directional linking** rather than hierarchical folders. Its goal is to create a "critical mass" of notes that begins to suggest new connections and ideas automatically (Ch 2, Ch 11).

### The 4 Note Types
1. **Fleeting Notes**: Quick reminders (discarded after processing).
2. **Literature Notes**: Brief gist of what you read + citation.
3. **Permanent Notes**: Self-contained units of thought, written in your own words, integrated into the box.
4. **Project Notes**: Specific to a current draft or outline (Ch 3).

### Bottom-Up Workflow
Avoid top-down planning. Let topics and arguments emerge from the **clusters** of notes you've naturally built up over time. This ensures you always have material to work with and stay aligned with your genuine interests (Ch 7, Ch 13).

### The Atomicity Principle
Each note should contain exactly **one idea**. This allows notes to be recombined, moved, and linked to different contexts without carrying irrelevant baggage (Ch 11).

### Writing as the Medium of Work
Writing is not the final step; it is the process of thinking. Every intellectual task—reading, research, observation—should produce a written byproduct (a note) (Ch 5).

### The Feedback Loop of Learning
Note-taking provides immediate feedback. If you can't translate an idea into your own words and link it to existing knowledge, you haven't fully understood it yet (Ch 10).
