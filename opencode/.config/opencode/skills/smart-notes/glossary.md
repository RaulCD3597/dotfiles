# Glossary of Smart Note-Taking

**Abstraction** — The process of extracting a general principle from a specific observation (Ch 12).

**Atomicity** — The principle that each note should contain exactly one idea to allow for maximum flexibility in linking (Ch 11).

**Bottom-Up** — A workflow where themes and structures emerge from the notes themselves, rather than being imposed from the top down (Ch 7).

**Collector's Fallacy** — The mistaken belief that acquiring information (bookmarks, books) is equivalent to learning (Ch 4).

**Elaboration** — The act of connecting new information to existing knowledge by explaining it in your own words (Ch 10).

**Fleeting Notes** — Temporary reminders or ideas captured on the fly, intended to be processed and discarded within 24–48 hours (Ch 3).

**Generation Effect** — The psychological finding that information is better remembered if it is actively produced (generated) by the person (Ch 10).

**GTD (Getting Things Done)** — David Allen's productivity system, which emphasizes capturing all tasks to free up working memory (Ch 1).

**Implementation Intention** — A "if-then" plan that links a situational cue to a specific behavior to help form habits (Ch 14).

**Index** — A high-level note that serves as an entry point into a specific cluster of notes (Ch 11).

**Literature Notes** — Brief, bibliographic notes on what you read, containing the gist of the author's argument and page references (Ch 3).

**Meno's Paradox** — The philosophical problem of how we can look for something if we don't know what it is (Ch 7).

**Note Sequence** — A chain of related notes that follow a specific "thread" of thought (Ch 15).

**Permanent Notes** — The core units of the slip-box; self-contained notes written in your own words and integrated into the network (Ch 3).

**Project Notes** — Temporary notes related to a specific writing project, such as outlines or drafts (Ch 3).

**Re-specification** — Taking an abstracted idea and applying it to a new, specific context (Ch 12).

**Slip-box (Zettelkasten)** — The central archive of interconnected permanent notes that acts as an external thinking tool (Ch 2).

**Survivorship Bias** — The error of focusing on the "winners" (successes) while ignoring the "losers" (failures) which are often more instructive (Ch 12).

**Verbund** — (Chemical industry term) A setup where the byproduct of one process becomes the resource for another (Ch 13).

**Zeigarnik Effect** — The tendency of the brain to stay occupied with incomplete tasks; writing things down "closes" the loop (Ch 8).
