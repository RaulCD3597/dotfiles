# Chapter 13: Share Your Insight

## Core Idea
The transition from notes to a manuscript should be a process of "editing" and "linearizing" what you've already built, not a struggle to fill a blank page.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Slip-box-storming**: Instead of brainstorming from scratch, you query your slip-box for existing clusters and connections.
- **The Linearization Process**: Turning a web of interconnected notes into a single, logical sequence for the reader.

## Key Concepts
- **Clusters as Signposts**: Large groups of notes indicate where you have the most material and interest; these become the core chapters of your work.
- **The Rough Draft**: A draft created by "translating" a sequence of notes into continuous text.
- **Kill Your Darlings**: The difficult task of removing interesting notes that don't fit the specific argument of the current paper.

## Mental Models
- **Writing as Revision**: You are never "writing" from scratch; you are always revising and connecting existing notes.
- **The Outliner as a Scaffolding**: Use a flexible structure that can change as the argument develops.

## Anti-patterns
- **Brainstorming**: Relying on the brain's "recency bias" instead of the slip-box's "relevance."
- **Clinging to a Plan**: Refusing to change the structure of a paper even when the notes suggest a better one.

## Key Takeaways
1. Look for where the "weight" of your notes is.
2. The manuscript is just another layer of the slip-box (project notes).
3. Work on multiple projects at different stages to avoid mental blocks.
4. Don't worry about being "original"; if you've done the work, originality will emerge from the connections.
