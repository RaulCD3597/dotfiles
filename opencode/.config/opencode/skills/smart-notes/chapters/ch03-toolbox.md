# Chapter 3: Everything You Need to Have

## Core Idea
The Zettelkasten requires very few tools, but they must be used consistently and for their specific purposes to prevent the system from becoming a mess.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 4 Note Types**:
    1. **Fleeting Notes**: Quick reminders or ideas caught "on the fly." (Discarded after processing).
    2. **Literature Notes**: Brief summaries of what you read, with bibliographic references.
    3. **Permanent Notes**: The "main" notes, written carefully in your own words and integrated into the slip-box.
    4. **Project Notes**: Temporary notes related to a specific piece of writing (e.g., drafts, outlines).

## Key Concepts
- **Bibliographic System**: A place to keep references and literature notes (e.g., Zotero, BibTeX).
- **The Slip-box (The Archive)**: The central place where permanent notes are stored and linked.
- **Outliner/Editor**: A tool for turning notes into a linear manuscript (e.g., Scrivener, Word).

## Mental Models
- **The Factory Verbund**: A setup where the byproduct of one task (e.g., reading a book) becomes the resource for another (e.g., writing an article).
- **Tool-Agnosticism**: The principles matter more than the software; whether paper or digital, the logic of linking remains the same.

## Anti-patterns
- **Mixing Note Types**: Storing fleeting notes in the same place as permanent notes, leading to "clutter" and loss of trust in the system.
- **The "Everything Collector"**: Collecting snippets and highlights without ever translating them into your own permanent notes.

## Key Takeaways
1. You only need a pen/paper or a simple text editor + a reference manager.
2. Fleeting notes are temporary and *must* be processed within a day or two.
3. Literature notes should be extremely brief—just enough to capture the gist and the location (page number).
4. Permanent notes must be written as if for publication, as they will be the building blocks of your future work.
